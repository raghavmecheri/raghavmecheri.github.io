Risk

Sep 28, 2026

In periods of rapid change, things are impossible until they aren't. The four-minute mile was a fact about the limits of the human body until the summer of 1954. Roger Bannister broke it in May, John Landy took the record off him 46 days later, and that August, the two of them broke four minutes in the same race in Vancouver. Nothing about anatomy changed in those three months — only consensus on what it was capable of. The Overton window opened, and those who were already running made it through. Everyone who started chasing the four-minute mile once Bannister proved it possible only got there once it was commonplace.

In periods of era-defining change, a step back from the edge is often expensive. Living on the miracle mile is genuinely unpleasant, it does not get less so with practice, and you are wrong about as often as you are early.

Discomfort is the only reliable evidence that you're taking enough risk. Nobody runs their fastest mile with their form intact through the tail. The only rule is that you keep running at the edge of what you can hold: go out hard, come up for air, or decide the barrier was never real. In times of rapid change, things feel impossible until everyone can do it.
