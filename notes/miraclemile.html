Miracle Mile

Jul 27, 2026

The four-minute mile was a fact about human limits until May of 1954. Landy took Bannister's record off him that June, and by August the two of them ran under four minutes in the same race in Vancouver. Nothing about human legs changed that summer. What changed is that the frontier stopped being a rumor and became a place you could stand on, and everyone who waited for it to resolve into something legible arrived to find it already priced. Exponential change runs that cycle every few months now, which means the position one step back from the edge is not the careful position: it is the late one. Living out there is genuinely unpleasant, and it does not get less unpleasant with practice. You spec a system for a furnace floor and the model underneath it is superseded before the install is done; you are wrong in front of a customer about as often as you are early. That discomfort is not the price of standing on the frontier, it is the only reliable evidence that you still are.

Nobody runs a fast mile with their form intact through the third lap, and you cannot hold that pace with your head up the whole way. The only rule is that you keep running at the edge of what you can hold: go out hard, come up for air, or decide the barrier was never real.
